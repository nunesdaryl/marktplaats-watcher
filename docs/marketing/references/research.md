# Context DNA: market research (10 October 2026)

## Job and audience
The buyer's job is to follow a specific second-hand item within a budget without repeatedly checking listings. This job is supported by the product's watch design and the [10 October evaluation's](../../../evals/report.md) Mac mini, Gazelle bicycle, iPhone 13, IKEA chair and Switch OLED cases. We have no interviews establishing how common each buyer type is. The [buyer personas](../generated/buyer-avatar.md) are hypotheses.

## Actual product and evidence
A watch checks on a user-selected schedule, scores new listings, and sends an email with a reason if the selected bar is met. It is not instant and it can miss matches. The 10 October scorer report's 53-listing sample gave 100% precision and 74% recall at the great threshold (median of three runs against corrected labels); at the good threshold, 86% precision and 78% recall. These are test-set results, not live customer rates. [Full methods and misses](../../../evals/report.md).

## Market language
The earlier desk-research notes were not first-party user interviews, so the campaign uses plain task language rather than presenting generic market phrases as customer quotes: “ik zoek een Mac mini met 16GB”, “maximaal €500”, “alleen goede matches”, “waarom kreeg ik deze melding?” English equivalents: “I’m looking for a Mac mini with 16GB”, “under €500”, “only good matches”, “why did I get this alert?” These are sample copy, not attributed speech.

## Alternatives and positioning
Marktplaats saved searches and third-party alert apps exist. [Competitor profiles](../competitors/) capture dated desk research, but competitor prices, speeds and feature claims can change. Position on the product's verifiable mechanism: the buyer chooses a schedule and alert bar; each email has a score and reason. Avoid “first”, “fastest”, “never miss”, or an unsupported superiority comparison.

## What research still needs
Interview users after a real first alert, ask what they sought and whether the alert was useful, and record permission before quoting. The [testimonial record](testimonials.md) has no approved public customer testimonial. No segmentation, savings, or conversion claim is established by current evidence.
